Brand elements

What every IAQ piece is built from, and when to use each part. It all comes out of the wordmark: IAQ is drawn in seven horizontal lines, so IAQ’s graphics are horizontal lines, squares and slim boxes, in red, white, black and navy. Measurements are read from IAQ’s own vector logo (2024.02.29) and measurement file; the file is named beside each one. Section 19 uses nothing that is not defined here, and section 03 holds the references it follows.

The lockup

IAQ’s own artwork, never rebuilt: the wordmark with “Your Total Facility Solutions Provider” set beneath it (IAQ, logo with tagline, 2024). The line is part of the artwork, so it is never retyped in another face.

Colour

Four colours, and IAQ is known for using both black and navy. The red and the black come from the logo file itself. Nothing yellow, ever: no amber, no gold, no warm filter.

The line

The line IAQ always uses: one long, thin, straight red line, left to right, across the whole piece or the whole column. It is the common use, and usually the only line on a piece: under a head or above a foot, once. It is refined, a quarter of one wordmark bar thick at the logo size on that piece. It is never separated, never short, and never repeated down a page: rows are held apart by space and tint, not a line each.

The seven lines

IAQ’s identity, used quietly: a small set of seven fine lines at the foot of a piece, full width. The lines swell toward the middle as the wordmark’s bars do, on open gaps, so the set reads as lines and never as a band: about three to five percent of the piece’s width tall. One set per piece, at the foot only, never behind type. The dense band and the five lines are retired.

The delivery cycle

The way IAQ presents its workflow, its design and its brand: six stages on one fine ring, the active stretch of the ring in red, each stage a white disc holding a grey isometric object, and one clean isometric visual at the centre that changes with the stage. Use it whenever IAQ explains how it works.

How it is drawn

  • The ring: one fine line, the one curved line IAQ draws; the stretch between the active stage and the next is red.
  • The discs: white, the same size, a soft lift and nothing else; the active disc takes a red ring.
  • The objects: grey isometric renders, one per stage, from the object set in section 06.
  • The centre: one isometric visual of the stage, white and grey with its red part, on white, looping quietly.
  • The labels: number and name in capitals beside each disc; the active one in red.
  • Motion: it steps to the next stage every three seconds until the reader touches it.
  • Never a second colour on the ring, a filled disc, or type on the centre visual.

Slim boxes and containers

IAQ contains things in slim horizontal boxes: black, red or navy with white type, or white with red type on a red ground. They are the buttons, the labels, the values in a table and the plates on a wall. Square corners, one line of type, at least four times as wide as they are tall.

The square and the texture

IAQ’s world is square. Markers are squares, textures are squares, figures can be built from squares. Round dots belong to other brands.

The construction wireframe

IAQ designs and engineers what it builds, so its pieces may show their construction: fine lines behind the layout, the rows of the wordmark, diagonals, a dimension. Horizontal and diagonal only, red at a third on white or white at a fifth on red and navy, never behind a sentence.

Visuals: when, and when not

A visual earns its place when it shows the real thing IAQ built, installed or runs. Otherwise the piece is type, lines and colour, which is what most of IAQ’s pieces are.

Use a visual

  • One per piece. A facility IAQ delivered, a cleanroom, plant, people at work.
  • As a clean block. Square corners, aligned to the margins, no fade, no frame.
  • In true colour, the yellow cast taken out and nothing else: tools/design-grade-0925.py.
  • Where no photograph exists, an isometric object or a drawn scene from section 06.

Leave it out

  • Never type on a visual. Type sits beside it, above it or below it.
  • Business cards, letterheads, signatures in replies, signage and plates: no photograph.
  • A figure or a statement is the visual: 250+, ISO Class 1.
  • Stock and generated pictures of work IAQ did not do.

Gradients

IAQ separates with strong blocks, squares and long lines, not with gradients. Colour meets colour at a clean edge.

Where a gradient may appear

  • Inside a photograph or a render: its own light, never added.
  • On screen, behind 3D and the dotted globe: the dark stage’s own falloff, where nothing is written.

Everywhere else, none

  • No fade from a photograph into a colour.
  • No gradient on a ground, a box, a button, a line, an icon or type.
  • No glow, no shadow on flat artwork.

Badges

The certification bodies’ own artwork, untouched and in full colour, and only on a white piece. A red or navy piece carries no badges, not even in a white container: they go on the white piece of the set, the letter, the card front, the last page. One height for all, in one row, at the foot of the piece, with the standards named once beside them.

Boundaries

The edges of the system, for design and for language. Anything outside them is not IAQ, however good it looks.

Design

  • No vertical line, of any weight, anywhere.
  • No short dash and no small block of stripes. Lines run the width.
  • No gradient, fade, glow or drop shadow on artwork.
  • No type on a photograph, and one photograph per piece at most.
  • No rounded corner, round dot or pill. Circles only where the thing is round.
  • No yellow, and no colour outside red, white, black and navy on a printed piece.
  • No blue, black or grey line.
  • No change to the wordmark: no recolour, no outline, no retyped line, never on a photograph.
  • No heavy type, 700 and above, and no rounded or soft faces.

Language

  • No dash as a pause or a separator. The middle dot joins facts; “to” writes a range.
  • No exclamation mark.
  • No negative statement in client facing copy: say what IAQ does.
  • No superlative and no claim without a source: figures first, published figures only.
  • No capitals for a sentence; capitals live only in slim boxes.
  • No ampersand in running sentences, only inside proper names.
  • No client name without IAQ’s permission, and no invented person or number.
  • Always sentence case, one idea per sentence, the reader’s benefit stated plainly.
