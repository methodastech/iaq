Isometric, detailed and simple

Every object is drawn at two levels from one model. The detailed drawing tells the story at illustration size; the simple one is the same object cut back to its main volumes, so it still reads at 32 px. The size it will show at decides the level, and a view never mixes the two.

The six services at both levels

✓Do

  • Detailed for illustration. Heroes, section heads and feature cards, at 160 px and up, one per view. It may run its one loop.
  • Simple for icons. Navigation, chips and small cards, at 32 to 64 px. Still in navigation and chips, with its one tiny motion only on a card that has nothing else on it.
  • One object, two levels. The simple icon is the detailed drawing cut back, never a new picture.
  • One level to a row. A row of icons is all simple. A row of illustrations is all detailed, at one size.
  • The same rules at both levels. True isometric, three greys, a fine edge, one red part, a clear ground.

✕Don’t

  • No detailed drawing at icon size. Below 160 px its fine detail closes up into a grey smudge.
  • No simple icon blown up. Above 64 px it has nothing left to say.
  • No mixing the levels in one row, one menu or one set.
  • No perspective or dimetric view, no second accent and no red on a second part.
  • No tile, box or disc behind an object, and no keyline round it.
  • No gradient, glow or shadow, and no motion that grows or shrinks.
