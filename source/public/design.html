      Visual identity

      The mark, the brand elements, colour, type, icons, photography and illustration: what every IAQ piece is made from.

      01

      The mark

      The wordmark is IAQ’s own file, used exactly as supplied and never redrawn.

      ✓Do

      • The primary file on light grounds, the file with the white ® on dark ones.
      • Clear space of x on all four sides, x being the height of the I; 72 px the smallest on screen.
      • In the header at 46 px, turning slowly, still on hover and still for reduced motion.

      ✕Don’t

      • No redrawing, retyping or restacking of the letters, and no mark built from dots or squares.
      • No full wordmark squeezed into a 32 px tab icon; small sizes need their own glyph.
      • No coloured or gradient plate behind the logo; it stands on white, black, navy or field red.

      The four files

      • Open: no small markThere is no monogram. A wordmark 668 by 276 px is scaled into a 32 px browser tab, where it can only be a smear. IAQ needs one glyph for small sizes, most likely the Q.
      • Open: an unused fileThe dimensional render is not referenced by any page. Either it gets a deliberate place, or it leaves the assets folder.
      • Header46 px tall, in a 600 px perspective, on a ten second idle turn. Reduced motion holds it at a fixed angle.
      • Clear space, sizes and misuseSet once, in Brand elements under The logo, measured, with every Don’t drawn.

      • No redrawing, respacing or retyping, and no line set in another face.

      Where the logo sits: at the top

      The IAQ opens every piece. It sits at the top: top left on the margins, or big across the top. Nothing on the piece sits above it, and it never sits at the foot, under the words or under a list. What the piece says comes after the name.

      ✓Do

      • Top left, on the margins, at the size the piece takes from the table above: covers, letters, panels, the directory.
      • Big across the top, margin to margin, where the piece is all brand: the folder, the totem, a hoarding.
      • Centred only on a business card back, with nothing above it and the business line at the foot.
      • Words, lists and pictures under it, in reading order: the name first, then what the piece says.
      • On a red block of its own only where the block is the top of what it sits in, as in the email signature’s banner.

      ✕Don’t

      • No words above the IAQ: no line, label or headline set over it.
      • No IAQ at the foot of a cover, a board or a list, and no IAQ block closing a piece like a sign off.
      • No IAQ under a photograph or under the figures.
      • No second IAQ at the foot to balance the one at the top.

      Brand colour

      Four colours and two reds. The bright IAQ red belongs to the logo, the line and small accents; the deeper field red covers large areas, where the bright red would glare. Nothing yellow, ever: no amber, no gold, no warm filter.

      ✓Do

      • IAQ red for the logo and the accents, field red for any large red area.
      • White words on field red, at every size.
      • A banner fade only: straight, one direction, black in multiply or white at 86%, eased over half the width.
      • Red, white, black and navy on every printed piece; the kind colours stay on screen.

      ✕Don’t

      • No bright red as a large field, and no recoloured logo or line.
      • No black words on red, and no pink tint of red as a ground.
      • No red in any gradient, no two stop fade with a seam, no grey veil and no organic blend.
      • No yellow, amber, gold or warm filter, and no navy filter over a photograph.

      Banner fades: the one gradient IAQ uses

      A photograph fades straight into black or white, in one direction, under the words. Black is laid in multiply, so the picture deepens rather than turning grey; white stops at 86%, so the picture still shows faintly behind the words. Both ease out over half the banner in ten or more stops, so there is no edge where the fade begins.

      Fonts

      Rigid and strong, never thick: Switzer for every name, headline, figure and label, Instrument Sans for sentences, the same two as the website. Word and Outlook fall back to Arial, the face of the line in the lockup. Both are free for commercial use: Switzer from Fontshare, Instrument Sans from Google Fonts.

      ✓Do

      • Switzer for names, headlines, figures and labels; Instrument Sans for sentences; Arial in Word and Outlook.
      • Three sizes on one surface, at most.
      • The floors: 7 pt in print, 6 pt for legal lines; 12 px on screen for labels, 16 px for sentences.
      • Sentence case; capitals only inside a slim box, three words at most.
      • One line, two balanced lines, or at least three words on the last line.

      ✕Don’t

      • No monospace or typewriter face, for anything.
      • No type below the floor, and no tiny tracked capitals as labels.
      • No fourth size on one surface.
      • No weight of 700 or above, and no rounded, script or condensed display face.
      • No runt: never one or two words alone on the last line of a heading, a lede or a paragraph.

      Every line ends well

      A line never ends with one or two words left on their own (a runt). A heading or a sentence either fits on one line, breaks into two balanced lines, or keeps at least three words on its last line. Rewrite, or move the break, until it does.

      The line

      One long, straight red line, margin to margin: the common line and usually the only one on a piece, once, under the head of a document or above the foot of a standing piece. It is 1.2% of the lockup’s width thick, a quarter of one wordmark bar.

      ✓Do

      • One line per piece, margin to margin, straight and whole: it starts exactly on the left margin and ends exactly on the right.
      • Equal margins, left and right; the text, the boxes and the pictures stay between the same two margins, except a photograph that bleeds.
      • 1.2% of the lockup’s width thick, in IAQ red, or white on red, navy and black.
      • Under the head on documents, above the foot on standing pieces.

      ✕Don’t

      • No short line, dash or tick as an accent.
      • No line broken into lengths, and no line under every row.
      • No vertical line, and no blue, black or grey line.

      The seven lines

      The wordmark’s seven rows, run full width at the foot: each line as thick as its bar, on the logo’s narrow gaps, the set as tall as the wordmark’s letters. Only on a piece with no logo, or at the foot of a big board; white only on IAQ red, red on every other ground.

      ✓Do

      • On a piece with no logo: a divider, a folder inside, a section opener.
      • On a big board, at the foot: a totem, hoarding or a booth wall, the set as tall as the wordmark’s letters there.
      • Drawn as the logo draws them: each line as thick as its bar, the gaps as narrow as the logo’s, never under 24 px tall on screen.
      • White only when the ground is IAQ red; red lines on every other ground: white, navy, grey or a photograph.

      ✕Don’t

      • No small set beside the logo, at the foot or the head of an ordinary piece.
      • No tall band in the middle of a board, and no thin lines on wide gaps: that is not the logo.
      • No small set where the lines merge, go grey or drop out.
      • No block of stripes used as a stamp, a flag or a sign off.
      • No white seven lines on navy, on white or over a photograph.

      The delivery cycle

      How IAQ shows the way it works, drawn one way everywhere, as on the live unit pages: six stations on one thin grey circle, the hand-offs in red, and the stage in view drawn large in the centre.

      ✓Do

      • Always six, always in order, clockwise from the top: Design, Procurement, Construction, Commissioning, Maintenance, Tools hookup.
      • The house isometric objects as the stations, standing on the ring with nothing round them.
      • One stage in view: its name in red at full strength, its object large in the centre; the others sit back.
      • The same drawing small, or as a row of the simple objects, never a different style.

      ✕Don’t

      • No disc, ring or box round a station, and no red ring on the one in view.
      • No old grey renders, no numbers and no capitals in the names.
      • No second colour or gradient on the ring, no dashes and no vertical timeline.
      • No growing or shrinking: the line draws on and the centre fades, nothing zooms.

      How it is drawn

      • The ring: one thin grey circle that stops short of every station, so no object sits on a line.
      • The hand-offs: in red with a plain chevron, solid up to the stage in view and a light red after it.
      • The stations: the house isometric line objects, one per stage, the same size, with no disc, ring or box.
      • The stage in view: its name in red and at full strength; its object drawn large in the centre with its name.
      • The labels: sentence case, Switzer 500, the full service name, no number.
      • Motion: the red line draws along the ring and the centre fades to the next stage; nothing grows or shrinks.

      The cycle in other places

      Slim boxes and containers

      IAQ contains things in slim horizontal boxes: black, red or navy with white type, or white with red type on a red ground. They are the buttons, the labels, the values in a table and the plates on a wall. Square corners, one line of type, at least four times as wide as they are tall.

      ✓Do

      • Square corners, one line, four times as wide as tall or wider.
      • Room inside: 0.62 em above and below, 1.05 em each side.
      • Black, red or navy with white words; white with red words on red.

      ✕Don’t

      • No rounded corner, pill or outline.
      • No two lines, no tall box, no square button.
      • No cramped words touching the edge of the box.

      The square and the texture

      IAQ’s world is square and clean: a square keys a map or a chart, and a figure can be built from squares. One calm, tone on tone square pattern is allowed as a background; no busy pattern sits behind the words.

      ✓Do

      • Squares, not dots, on a navy or black field, red or white.
      • The square pattern as a background, tone on tone, large squares on narrow gaps, a sixtieth of the width.
      • A figure at least 14 squares tall per character, more for a large piece.
      • Published figures only.

      ✕Don’t

      • No logo made of squares or dots.
      • No coarse grid that turns a figure into steps.
      • No square as a bullet, no round dots, and no dotted grid, perforation or checker.
      • No loud pattern: the square pattern stays one tone on its ground, never two colours.

      The construction wireframe

      IAQ designs and engineers what it builds, so its pieces may show their construction: fine lines behind the layout, the rows of the wordmark, diagonals, a dimension. Horizontal and diagonal only, red at a third on white or white at a fifth on red and navy, never behind a sentence.

      • Horizontal and diagonal only, red at a third on white, white at a fifth on red and navy.
      • On the wordmark’s row rhythm, with a dimension where it helps.

      ✕Don’t

      • No vertical line in the construction.
      • Never behind a sentence, and never dashed.

      Visuals: when, and when not

      A visual earns its place when it shows the real thing IAQ built, installed or runs. Otherwise the piece is type, lines and colour, which is what most of IAQ’s pieces are. Type may sit on a photograph only inside a solid block.

      ✓Do

      • One visual per piece: a facility IAQ delivered, a cleanroom, plant, people at work.
      • As a clean block, square corners, aligned to the margins or bleeding off the page.
      • In true colour, the yellow cast taken out and nothing else.
      • Type on it only inside a solid block; where no photograph exists, an isometric object.

      ✕Don’t

      • No type straight on a photograph.
      • No photograph on business cards, letterheads, envelopes, signage or plates.
      • No stock picture of work IAQ did not do, and no generated scene shown as real.
      • No close up of faces where the work should show; no client or supplier mark in frame.

      Labels on a photograph

      When a photograph carries a label, the label is a slim box set flush into the photograph’s top left corner: no gap, square corners, one line. The corner holds it, so it reads as part of the picture’s frame, not a sticker floating on it.

      ✓Do

      • Flush to the top left corner, no gap, on every photograph that carries a label.
      • A slim box, square, one line, three words at most.
      • One label per picture, and the longer words under the picture, not on it.

      ✕Don’t

      • No inset label with a gap to the corner, and no label floating mid picture.
      • No rounded pill, outline or shadow.
      • No label in another corner, and never two labels on one picture.

      Layout and spacing

      Space is what makes an IAQ piece look engineered. Everything hangs from one left margin, gaps come from one scale, and nothing touches an edge. When a piece feels crowded, take something out; never shrink the gaps.

      ✓Do

      • One left margin, the same on every page of a set.
      • Gaps from the scale, larger for a larger change of subject.
      • Room at the edges: margins of at least a twentieth of the width.

      ✕Don’t

      • No crowding: take something out rather than shrink a gap.
      • No ragged left edges and no block centred among left aligned ones.
      • No element touching an edge, except a photograph that bleeds on purpose.

      Gradients

      IAQ separates with strong blocks, squares and long lines. One gradient is allowed, and only on a banner: a straight fade from a photograph into black or white, under the words. The examples and the curve are in Brand colour, under banner fades.

      ✓Do

      • A straight fade, one direction, from the photograph into black or white, on a banner or a standing piece only.
      • Black in multiply at 94%, white at 86%, under the words.
      • Eased over half the width in ten or more stops, so no seam shows.
      • Inside a photograph or a render: its own light, never added.

      ✕Don’t

      • No red, navy or any colour in a gradient.
      • No two stop fade with a visible edge, and no grey veil over the whole picture.
      • No organic gradient: radial, blurred, hazy, a sky blend or a photograph melting away.
      • No gradient on a ground, a box, a button, a line, an icon or type, and no glow or shadow on flat artwork.

      Badges

      The certification bodies’ own artwork, untouched and in full colour, on the white piece of a set only: one height, one row, at the foot, with the standards named once beside them. A red or navy piece carries none, not even in a white box.

      ✓Do

      • Badges together, one height, a quarter of that height apart, in one row at the foot.

      ✕Don’t

      • No badges spread across the width, and no more than four in a row.
      • No badge on red, navy or a photograph, not even in a white box; no tint, no outline.

      Boundaries

      The edges of the system, for design and for language. Anything outside them is not IAQ, however good it looks.

      Design

      • No vertical line, of any weight, anywhere.
      • No short dash and no small block of stripes. Lines run the width.
      • No gradient, except a straight fade into black in multiply or white at 86% on a banner, eased with no seam; never red, never organic; no glow or drop shadow.
      • No bright red as a large field: covers, backs, stands and signs take field red, #C8161D.
      • No label floating on a photograph: it sits flush in the top left corner.
      • No type straight on a photograph; on a solid block only, and one photograph per piece at most.
      • No rounded corner, round dot or pill. Circles only where the thing is round.
      • No yellow, and no colour outside red, white, black and navy on a printed piece.
      • No blue, black or grey line.
      • No change to the wordmark: no recolour, no outline, no retyped line, never on a photograph.
      • No heavy type, 700 and above, no rounded or soft faces, and no monospace or typewriter face.
      • No small set of seven lines beside the logo; on a big board only at its foot, drawn as the logo draws its rows.
      • No busy pattern: no dotted grid, perforation or checker; the square pattern only, tone on tone.
      • No icon in a box, tile, card or disc, arrows included.
      • No logo in dots or squares, and no figure in squares under 14 squares tall.
      • No numbering such as 01, 02, 03 or 1, 2, 3 in front of items.
      • No runt: no line ending with one or two words alone; one line, two balanced lines, or three words on the last.
      • No uneven margins: left and right the same, the line exactly between them.
      • No tilted mockups; pieces are shown straight.

      Language

      • No dash as a pause or a separator; “to” writes a range.
      • No dots or bullets: no middle dot between items and no bullet list; commas, line breaks and rows instead.
      • No exclamation mark.
      • No negative statement in client facing copy: say what IAQ does.
      • No superlative and no claim without a source: figures first, published figures only.
      • No capitals for a sentence; capitals live only in slim boxes.
      • No ampersand in running sentences, only inside proper names.
      • No client name without IAQ’s permission, and no invented person or number.
      • Always sentence case, one idea per sentence, the reader’s benefit stated plainly.

      Grid and space

      Every page runs on one container, one margin and twelve window widths where the layout changes shape.

      ✓Do

      • Read the two tokens. The width comes from --maxw and the side margin from --gut, never from a typed number.
      • Let the band own the margin. The band carries --gut as its own padding; the element inside it carries the width.
      • Give every grid track a zero floor, minmax(0, 1fr), so one long word cannot push a page sideways.
      • Put any change to the shell on one of the five main stops: 640, 760, 820, 900 or 960.
      • Divide screen maths by the zoom. From 1025px up, a size in vh or vw that has to match the screen divides by --zoomf.

      ✕Don’t

      • No typed margin, no 20px or 2rem where --gut belongs.
      • No borrowed wrapper: a band never leans on .wrap for its side padding.
      • No auto minimum on a grid track, and no 280px card floor without min(100%, 280px) around it.
      • No new width stop for the shell. A component’s own stop moves only its own tracks.
      • No bare 100vh from 1025px up: in a 900px window it paints 1008px tall and pushes the page down.

      Technical notes for the developer

      The two tokens

      Container, --maxw: min(100%, 1900px). The shell runs edge to edge on the client’s instruction of 25 August, take the whole space left and right. The min() only bites on an ultrawide monitor, so a line of prose never stretches past reading length.

      Gutter, --gut: clamp(14px, 1.5vw, 26px). The smallest gutter that still keeps type off the bezel. It is a token, never a literal: every band on every page resolves its left and right padding from this one declaration.

      Above 1600px, clamp(24px, 4.4vw − 46px, 64px). It replaced the flat 26px cap on 4 September. Nothing was misaligned: 26px is 1.3% of a 2000px screen, so on a large monitor the whole site read as printed to the edge of the paper. The formula is continuous at the breakpoint, 24px from the old rule against 24.4px from the new one, so dragging a window across 1600px shows no step. It is declared twice, on :root and on body.page-home, because the home page redeclares the token at its own specificity and would otherwise ignore the base rule.

      At 640px and under, :root { --gut: 14px } and body.page-home { --gut: 16px }. Locked to a pixel rather than left fluid: below this width the clamp floor is already in force, so a formula would only be a slower way of writing 14.

      Structure

      Who owns what. .pg-sec { padding: X var(--gut) Y } and .pg-in { max-width: var(--maxw) }: the band owns the horizontal gutter, the inner element owns the width. Written into the shared page stylesheet as a standing rule after a two class selector outranked .head.wrap and flattened a page against the screen edge. No band in the phase two system depends on .wrap.

      Every track, minmax(0, …). No grid track in the shared page system is allowed an implicit auto minimum. An auto minimum is what lets one long word push a whole page sideways.

      Two section systems, .sec and .pg-sec. The home page runs .sec from its own stylesheet. The other eighteen pages import the shared phase two stylesheet and run .pg-sec. Both read the same tokens and the numbers differ, which is why the padding drawing lists them separately.

      The width stops. Five max-width values, 640, 760, 820, 900 and 960, account for 134 of the 183 max-width queries. That is the honest reading of the stylesheets, not a scale designed in advance. A component breakpoint that only moves its own tracks is safe; anything that moves the shell belongs on one of the five.

      The grids in detail

      Scroll assembly, .fab-in: minmax(300px, .38fr) 1fr, gap clamp(24px, 4vw, 64px). The 300px floor is why the rail never crushes into an unreadable strip before the 900px breakpoint stacks it.

      The step scale nobody reads

      --s1 through --s8, at 8, 14, 20, 28, 38, 52, 70 and 96px, sit on the home page body, and six of the eight are redeclared smaller at 640px. No rule in the build reads any of them. The rhythm is carried by clamp() on the band instead, because a fixed step cannot answer the viewport and section padding is the one measurement that has to. Read the clamps, not the scale.

      The trap the zoom carries

      Viewport units on this site do not measure the viewport. The zoom sits on the root element, and viewport units resolve against the unzoomed window before the zoom scales the result. A stage declared 100vh paints 1008px tall in a 900px window and pushes the page down by the difference. Anything sized in vh, vw, svh or dvh that has to match the real screen divides by --zoomf.

      .fab-stage { height: calc(100svh / var(--zoomf, 1)); }.hero { min-height: min(calc((86svh − var(--tbh, 0px)) / var(--zoomf, 1.12)), 786px); }

      The fallbacks differ on purpose. .fab-stage is declared outside any media query, so below 1025px the fallback of 1 is the value that applies. The hero rule lives inside the 1025px query, so its 1.12 fallback never fires and only names the tier it sits in. The 786px cap is one number written twice: 786 multiplied by 1.12 is the 880 painted pixels the hero is allowed, set after the client said the banner was too tall for a website.

      The same trap has two more faces. Script has to read the zoom, never assume it. There are three tiers, so the /home2 route reads getComputedStyle(document.documentElement).zoom and republishes the true viewport as --vpw and --vph before its scroll triggers measure anything. And a bounding rect is not a CSS pixel. getBoundingClientRect() returns painted pixels, while a value written back into top or paddingTop is read inside the zoom and scaled a second time. Measuring the workspace bar with the rect left an 8px strip of page showing between the bar and the nav, which is why that bar is measured with offsetHeight.

      Motion

      Things fade in and rise a little on one house curve, lines draw along their length, nothing zooms, and the page then rests.

      No zoom

      Nothing grows or shrinks on an IAQ screen. A photograph, a card, a menu, a marker or the 3D facility never zooms in or out by itself, on hover, on scroll or on arrival. Things arrive by fading and by a short rise, lines draw along their own length, and the page then rests.

      ✓Do

      • Fade and rise. Opacity with a rise of 12 to 18 px, on the house curve, once.
      • Draw lines along their length. A red line, a bar or a progress fill may grow along itself, which is drawing, not zoom.
      • Hold the photograph. It wipes in or fades in at its final size and stays there.
      • Leave zoom to the reader. The 3D facility keeps its own plus and minus, and a pinch on a phone.

      ✕Don’t

      • No hover zoom on a photograph, a card or a thumbnail, however slight.
      • No settle, a picture arriving at 104% and easing to 100%.
      • No scale reveal, a block arriving from 96% or 98.5%.
      • No pulse, a marker, pin or button swelling and shrinking.
      • No menu or panel opening with a scale.
      • No camera push in, the 3D view easing closer by itself.

      04

      No eyebrow directly above a heading.

      A small uppercase label sitting on top of a title does the title’s job twice: by the time the eye reaches the heading it has already read a quieter version of it.       Technical notes

      The gutter is overridden twice. From 1600px up it becomes clamp(24px, 4.4vw - 46px, 64px). The 26px cap was 1.3% of a 2000px screen, so a large monitor read as printed to the edge of the paper. At exactly 1600px both rules give 24px, so dragging a window across the breakpoint shows no step. At 640px and below it is a flat 14px, the floor stated rather than computed. The 1600px rule also names body.page-home, because the home page redefines the gutter at a higher specificity and :root alone would have been ignored there.

      Technical notes
